Research

My work has three threads. One makes AI systems safer, and the other two use AI to understand biology.

🤖 Multi-agent LLM safety

LLM agents are increasingly deployed in teams. When they share a goal that conflicts with ours, they can coordinate covertly, and their outputs alone may not show it. I study how to detect this collusion from the models' internal activations, and how to assign credit fairly when agents work together.

PhD · in progress

Sequential collusion probes

Extends NARCBench, a benchmark that detects collusion between LLM agents with activation probes, from single messages to whole conversations. Adds trajectory-level probes, per-round layer selection, and a benchmark comparing trajectory probes with simple averaging.

🎯 AI for cancer immunotherapy

Personalised cancer vaccines teach the immune system to recognise a patient's own tumour. The hard part is choosing which antigens to target. I build computational tools for finding them: genomics pipelines, binding predictors and, more recently, LLM-based systems.

Whole-genome ERV insertion discovery

Endogenous retroviruses (ERVs) are ancient viral DNA in our genome. When they reactivate in tumours, they can be a source of antigens. This Snakemake workflow takes paired tumour whole-genome FASTQs and calls candidate non-reference ERV insertions with ERVcaller. It is a new module in a pipeline for ERV-derived antigens.

Peptide–HLA binding prediction

Deep learning models that use 3D structural features to predict which tumour peptides bind a patient's HLA molecules, a key step in choosing vaccine targets. Our module outperformed NetMHCpan 4.

🧬 Virtual cells & gene regulatory networks

If we could predict how a cell responds when a gene is switched off, we could screen therapies on a computer before testing them in the lab. Most perturbation models predict well but work as black boxes. I build models that also learn the gene regulatory network (GRN) behind the prediction.

Master's research · Macquarie University

A virtual-cell framework for predicting gene perturbations through regulatory network inference

Extends GEARS with a signed GRN encoder trained with a multi-task loss (expression error + sparsity + multi-hop regulation), so the model predicts post-perturbation expression and learns an interpretable network at the same time. It improves prediction for the genes that matter most, the differentially expressed ones.
